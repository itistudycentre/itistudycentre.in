PART 1 · MEASUREMENT

1. Linear Measurement

Linear Measurement is the measurement of length. The base unit of length in the SI system is the Metre (m).

In engineering practice, the preferred unit is the Millimetre (mm). 1 m = 1000 mm.

UnitEquivalent
1 Metre (m)1000 mm
1 Centimetre (cm)10 mm
1 Millimetre (mm)1000 µm
1 Micrometre (µm)0.001 mm

Steel Rule

Engineer's steel rules are used to measure dimensions of jobs. They are made of spring steel or stainless steel. Available in lengths 150mm, 300mm, 600mm. Reading accuracy: 0.5 mm and 1/64 inch.

📌 Exam Focus

SI base unit of length: Metre (m). Engineering preference: Millimetre (mm).

PART 2 · MARKING TOOLS

2. Scribers

A Scriber is a marking tool used to draw lines on metal surfaces. It is made of high carbon steel, hardened and tempered.

  • Straight Scriber: Most common type.
  • Bent Scriber: Used for marking in difficult-to-reach areas.
  • Scratch Awl: Similar to a scriber, used for leather and wood.
⚠ Safety

Scriber points are very sharp. Do not carry in pocket. Use a cork to cover the point when not in use.

PART 3 · CALIPERS

3. Calipers

Calipers are indirect measuring tools used to transfer measurements from a steel rule to the job, and vice versa.

Types of Calipers

  • Firm Joint Calipers: Legs are set by tapping on wood.
  • Spring Joint Calipers: Have an adjusting nut for quick setting.
  • Inside Calipers: For measuring internal dimensions.
  • Outside Calipers: For measuring external dimensions.
  • Jenny Calipers (Hermaphrodite): One leg with a point, used for marking parallel lines and finding centres.
💡 Note

Spring joint calipers have the advantage of quick setting using the adjusting nut.

PART 4 · PUNCHES

4. Types of Marking Punches

  • Centre Punch: Point angle 90°. Used to mark hole centres. Gives a wide mark for drill starting.
  • Prick Punch (Dot Punch): Point angle 30° or 60°. 30° used for divider placement; 60° for witness marks.
📌 Exam Focus

Centre Punch angle: 90°  |  Prick Punch angle: 30° or 60°.

PART 5 · HOLDING TOOLS

5. Bench Vice

A Bench Vice is used to hold workpieces during filing, sawing, threading, and other operations.

Made of cast iron or cast steel. Size is specified by the width of the jaws (e.g., 150 mm bench vice).

Parts of a Bench Vice

  • Fixed Jaw
  • Movable Jaw
  • Hardened Steel Jaws
  • Spindle
  • Handle
  • Box-nut & Spring
💡 Good Practice

Use soft jaws (vice clamps) made of aluminium over regular jaws to protect finished surfaces.

PART 6 · VICES

6. Types of Vices

  • Quick Releasing Vice: Has a trigger for fast jaw adjustment.
  • Pipe Vice: Used for holding pipes and tubes. Grips at four points.
  • Hand Vice: For holding small jobs like taps, rivets, and small drills.
  • Pin Vice: For holding small diameter jobs with a collet chuck.
  • Toolmaker's Vice: For precision work, filing, drilling on surface plates.
  • Leg Vice: Used in forge shops for bending and forging. Made of mild steel.
PART 7 · SQUARENESS

7. Try Square

A Try Square is used to check the squareness (90° angle) of a surface. It has a blade fixed at 90° to the stock.

Accuracy: approximately 0.002 mm per 10 mm length. Specified by blade length: 100 mm, 150 mm, 200 mm.

Uses

  • Checking squareness of surfaces.
  • Checking flatness.
  • Marking lines at 90° on workpieces.
  • Setting workpieces at right angles.
PART 8 · FILES

8. Types of Files

Files are used to remove excess material from workpieces. They are classified by shape, cut, and grade.

File Cuts

  • Single Cut: One row of teeth. For soft metals (brass, aluminium, copper).
  • Double Cut: Two rows of teeth. Faster material removal.
  • Rasp Cut: Individual sharp teeth. For wood, leather.
  • Curved Cut: Deep cutting action. For soft materials like aluminium, tin, plastic.

File Grades (Teeth per 10 mm)

File Shapes

  • Flat File: Rectangular cross-section. General purpose.
  • Hand File: Similar to flat, but one edge is safe (no teeth).
  • Square File: Square cross-section. For keyways and slots.
  • Round File: Circular cross-section. For enlarging round holes.
  • Half-Round File: Semi-circular. For internal curved surfaces.
  • Triangular File: Triangular cross-section. For angles greater than 60°.
  • Knife Edge File: Sharp triangular. For narrow slots and angles.
📌 Exam Focus

Pinning: Metal chips stuck between teeth. Remove with a file card or brass/copper strip.

PART 9 · ANGULAR MEASUREMENT

9. Measurement of Angles

A full circle is divided into 360 degrees (°). Each degree = 60 minutes (′), each minute = 60 seconds (″).

Angular Measuring Instruments

  • Bevel Gauge: Indirect measurement, used with a protractor.
  • Universal Bevel Gauge: Has an additional blade for measuring angles not possible with a simple bevel gauge.
  • Bevel Protractor: Direct measurement, accuracy 1°.
  • Combination Set: Includes protractor head, square head, centre head, and rule. Used for layout, measurement, and angle checking.
PART 10 · SURFACE GAUGES

10. Surface Gauges

A Surface Gauge (or Scribing Block) is used to draw lines parallel to a datum surface, set jobs on machines, check height and parallelism.

Types

  • Fixed Type: Heavy flat base, vertical spindle with a scriber.
  • Universal Type: Spindle adjustable to any position, fine adjustment, can be used on cylindrical surfaces.
💡 Note

Universal surface gauge has a guide pin to scribe lines parallel to any datum edge.

PART 11 · CHISELS

11. Cold Chisel

A Cold Chisel is a hand cutting tool used for chipping and cutting operations. Made of high carbon steel or chrome vanadium steel.

Common Types

  • Flat Chisel: For large flat surfaces, removing excess metal.
  • Cross-cut (Cape) Chisel: For cutting keyways, grooves, and slots.
  • Half-Round Nose Chisel: For cutting curved grooves (oil grooves).
  • Diamond Point Chisel: For squaring corners.
  • Web (Punching) Chisel: For separating metals after chain drilling.

Chisel Angles

⚠ Safety

Do not use a mushroom-headed chisel. Grind it properly. Always use safety goggles while chipping.

PART 12 · MARKING MEDIA

12. Marking Media

Marking media are applied to workpieces to make scribed lines clearly visible.

Common Marking Media

  • White Wash: Chalk powder mixed with water, methylated spirit, or turpentine. For rough castings and forgings.
  • Marking Blue: Chemical dye mixed with methylated spirit. For machined surfaces.
  • Prussian Blue: For filed or machine-finished surfaces. Gives very clear lines but takes longer to dry.
  • Copper Sulphate: Mixed with water and nitric acid. For filed or machined surfaces. Toxic – handle with care.
  • Cellulose Lacquer: Commercially available, quick drying, various colours.
PART 13 · SURFACE PLATE

13. Surface Plate

A Surface Plate provides a perfectly flat datum surface for accurate marking and inspection. Made of high-quality cast iron or granite.

Three grades: Grade 1, 2, 3. Grade 1 is most accurate.

💡 Care

Clean surface plate before and after use. Protect with a thin layer of oil after cleaning.

PART 14 · ANGLE PLATES

14. Angle Plate

An Angle Plate has two flat surfaces machined perfectly at 90° to each other. Made of fine-grained cast iron or steel.

Types

  • Plain Solid Angle Plate: Most common. Two flat surfaces at 90°.
  • Slotted Type Angle Plate: Has slots for clamping bolts.
  • Swivel Type Angle Plate: Adjustable angle between surfaces.
  • Box Angle Plate: All faces square to each other; job can be turned.

Grades: Grade 1 (high precision) and Grade 2 (general shop work).

PART 15 · PROPERTIES

15. Physical and Mechanical Properties of Metals

Physical Properties

  • Colour: Different metals have distinct colours.
  • Weight / Specific Gravity: Varies by metal.
  • Structure: Fibrous or granular.
  • Conductivity: Thermal and electrical.
  • Magnetic Property: Ferrous metals are magnetic, non-ferrous are not.
  • Fusibility: Melting point.

Mechanical Properties

  • Ductility: Can be drawn into wire (e.g., copper, aluminium).
  • Malleability: Can be hammered or rolled into sheets (e.g., lead).
  • Hardness: Resistance to indentation.
  • Brittleness: Breaks without permanent deformation (e.g., cast iron).
  • Toughness: Can withstand impact (e.g., wrought iron).
  • Tenacity: Resistance to tensile forces (e.g., mild steel).
  • Elasticity: Returns to original shape after force removal (e.g., springs).
PART 16 · SAWS

16. Metal-Cutting Saws

  • Power Saw: Most common. Reciprocating action.
  • Horizontal Band-Saw: Continuous cutting, faster than power saw.
  • Circular Saw: For large cross-sections. Continuous cutting.
  • Contour Saw: For cutting irregular profiles. Uses a metal band saw blade.
⚠ Safety

Always use cutting fluid. Do not apply excessive cutting pressure. Use stop gauge for multiple pieces of same length.

PART 17 · MICROMETER

17. Outside Micrometer

An Outside Micrometer is a precision instrument for measuring dimensions up to 0.01 mm accuracy.

Parts

  • Frame: Drop-forged steel or malleable cast iron.
  • Barrel / Sleeve: Fixed to frame; has datum line and graduations.
  • Thimble: Has graduations; connected to spindle.
  • Spindle: Moves forward/backward via screw mechanism.
  • Anvil: Fixed measuring face.
  • Spindle Lock Nut: Locks spindle in position.
  • Ratchet Stop: Ensures uniform pressure.

Least Count (Metric)

Spindle pitch = 0.5 mm. Thimble has 50 divisions. Least count = 0.5/50 = 0.01 mm.

📌 Exam Focus

Reading a micrometer: Barrel reading (mm) + Thimble reading (number of divisions × 0.01).

PART 18 · DEPTH MICROMETER

18. Depth Micrometer

A Depth Micrometer is used to measure the depth of holes, slots, and recesses.

It has a stock with a graduated sleeve, a thimble, and interchangeable extension rods. Least count: 0.01 mm.

Uses

  • Measuring depth of blind holes.
  • Measuring depth of slots and grooves.
  • Measuring height of shoulders or projections.
PART 19 · VERNIER

19. Vernier Calipers

A Vernier Caliper is a precision measuring instrument with accuracy up to 0.02 mm.

Parts

  • Fixed Jaws: Part of the beam scale; one for outside, one for inside measurement.
  • Movable Jaws: Part of the vernier slide.
  • Vernier Slide: Slides over the beam; can be set using thumb lever.
  • Beam: Main scale graduations.
  • Depth Bar: Attached to vernier slide; measures depth.
  • Vernier Scale: Graduations on the vernier slide.

Least Count

10 VSD = 9 mm (MSD = 1 mm). Least count = 1 mm - 9/10 mm = 0.1 mm (for 10-division vernier). For 50-division vernier: 50 VSD = 49 mm, LC = 0.02 mm.

💡 Note

Never use a vernier caliper for any purpose other than measuring. Clean and store in box after use.

PART 20 · BEVEL PROTRACTOR

20. Vernier Bevel Protractor

A Vernier Bevel Protractor is a precision instrument for measuring angles with accuracy of 5′ (5 minutes).

Parts

  • Stock: Contact surface for measuring angle.
  • Dial: Integral part of the stock; graduated in degrees.
  • Blade: Second contact surface; can be extended.
  • Locking Screws: Lock dial and blade.

Least Count

23 MSD = 12 VSD. 1 MSD = 1°. LC = 2° - (23/12)° = 5′.

PART 21 · DIAL CALIPER

21. Dial Caliper

A Dial Caliper is a direct-reading instrument similar to a vernier caliper but faster and easier to read.

The beam scale is graduated in 5 mm increments. The dial is divided into 100 equal parts. Each dial graduation represents 5/100 = 0.05 mm.

💡 Digital Caliper

Digital calipers have an LCD/LED display, accuracy up to 0.01 mm. Features include zero setting, metric/inch conversion, and auto power-off.

PART 22 · DRILLING

22. Drilling Processes – Drilling Machines

Drilling Machine Types

  • Sensitive Bench Drilling Machine: For light-duty work up to 12.5 mm diameter.
  • Pillar Drilling Machine: Larger version, floor-mounted, more powerful.
  • Column Drilling Machine: For heavy work.
  • Radial Drilling Machine: Arm can swing, drill can cover large work area.
  • Gang Drilling Machine: Multiple spindles on a common table for sequential operations.
  • Multiple Spindle Head Drilling Machine: Many spindles driven by one head; for mass production.

Work-Holding Devices

  • Machine Vice: For most drilling jobs. Use parallels to elevate the job.
  • Clamps and Bolts: Use T-slot bolts on the machine table to secure large jobs.
📌 Exam Focus

Drill point angle: Standard 118° for general purpose. Varies with material.

PART 23 · TAPS & WRENCHES

23. Hand Taps and Wrenches

Hand Taps are used to cut internal threads. Made of high carbon steel or HSS.

Types of Taps in a Set

  • Taper Tap (First): For starting the thread.
  • Intermediate Tap (Second): For continuing the thread.
  • Bottoming Tap (Plug): For finishing threads to full depth in blind holes.

Tap Wrenches

  • Double-Ended Adjustable Tap Wrench: Most common, for larger taps.
  • T-Handle Tap Wrench: For restricted spaces, one-hand operation.
  • Solid Type Tap Wrench: Non-adjustable, for specific tap sizes.

Tap Drill Size

Tap drill size = Major diameter - Pitch (for ISO metric threads). Example: M10 × 1.5 → drill size = 10 - 1.5 = 8.5 mm.

⚠ Important

Always use the correct tap drill size. Too small – tap may break; too large – weak threads.

📌 Module 2 – सबसे महत्वपूर्ण बातें

  • Linear measurement in engineering is primarily in millimetres.
  • Scriber points are sharp; handle with care.
  • Spring joint calipers offer quick setting via adjusting nut.
  • Centre punch (90°) for hole centres; Prick punch (30°/60°) for divider placement and witness marks.
  • Bench vice size is specified by jaw width.
  • Try square checks 90° squareness and flatness.
  • Files are specified by shape, cut, and grade.
  • Chisel point angle varies with material: mild steel 55°, cast iron 60°, etc.
  • Outside micrometer least count is 0.01 mm.
  • Vernier caliper least count is 0.02 mm.
  • Drilling machine types include sensitive bench, pillar, radial, gang, and multiple spindle.
  • Hand taps come in sets of three: taper, intermediate, bottoming.
  • Tap drill size for ISO metric = Major diameter – Pitch.
